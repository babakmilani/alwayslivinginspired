The Coastal Grandmother Grows Up: How Elevated Resort Wear Is Defining the Most Sophisticated Aesthetic of 2025

By The Fashion Editor | July 27, 2026

This fashion trends guide explores the latest trends and styling strategies. Resort wear has undergone a breathtaking transformation, shedding its casual vacation roots to emerge as one of the most refined and culturally resonant aesthetics of 2025. From linen-draped silhouettes to sun-bleached palettes reimagined in luxurious fabrics, elevated resort dressing is no longer confined to poolside lounging — it has arrived, confidently, on city streets, boardrooms, and evening terraces worldwide. This definitive guide unpacks everything you need to know about mastering this effortlessly sophisticated movement.

There is a particular kind of woman — and man — who moves through the world with the quiet confidence of someone who has just returned from a long, unhurried afternoon on a sunlit terrace overlooking the Mediterranean. They aren't necessarily tan, and they aren't necessarily on vacation. What they are, undeniably, is impeccably dressed in elevated resort wear — the defining aesthetic that has quietly conquered 2025's most discerning wardrobes.

What began as a niche internet appreciation for the "Coastal Grandmother" archetype — think Diane Keaton in Something's Gotta Give, crisp white linen and wide-brimmed hats — has evolved into something far more layered, far more ambitious, and far more commercially significant. Today's elevated resort aesthetic borrows from that DNA but pushes it decisively forward, incorporating precision tailoring, unexpected fabric combinations, global craft traditions, and a fearless approach to accessories that turns every outfit into a considered, complete story.

The timing is not accidental. As fashion collectively exhaled from several years of maximalism, logomania, and performative streetwear, a deep hunger emerged for clothing that felt simultaneously relaxed and refined. Resort wear — with its inherent ease, its romance with natural light, and its long-standing love affair with beautiful textiles — was perfectly positioned to answer that call. What changed was the execution: the casualness was refined, the romance was elevated, and the result is a trend that is as relevant in a London gallery opening as it is at a Capri cliff-side restaurant.

Understanding the Elevated Resort Aesthetic: More Than Just Vacation Clothes

To understand why elevated resort wear has become such a dominant force in 2025, it helps to first dismantle the assumptions that have historically limited the category. For too long, resort wear was dismissed as a niche seasonal offering — pretty, perhaps, but ultimately frivolous. Cruise collections from luxury houses were treated as bonus capsules rather than serious fashion statements. That perception has been thoroughly dismantled.

What elevated resort wear represents in its current form is a philosophy of dressing as much as a collection of garments. At its heart, the aesthetic prizes three interconnected values: quality of material, generosity of silhouette, and intentionality of detail. A wide-leg trouser in washed silk is not simply comfortable — it is a statement about how its wearer relates to their body and their time. A hand-embroidered kaftan is not just decorative — it speaks to a reverence for craft and an understanding that clothing can carry cultural meaning without appropriating it.

The key figures driving this evolution include designers like Loro Piana, Totême, Gabriela Hearst, and Mara Hoffman, alongside a new generation of independent labels operating out of the Greek islands, the Alentejo coast of Portugal, and coastal Mexico — all of whom are treating resort collections with the same conceptual rigor previously reserved for runway presentations. Their collective influence has elevated the conversation, and their customers have responded with enormous enthusiasm.

The Fabric Foundations: What Elevated Resort Wear Is Actually Made Of

If there is a single element that most clearly distinguishes elevated resort wear from its budget-holiday counterpart, it is the extraordinary attention paid to fabric selection. This is a category where material is not merely a backdrop for design — it is the design itself.

Linen remains the undisputed sovereign of the elevated resort palette, but today's linen is nothing like the stiff, wrinkle-prone fabric that plagued previous generations. Belgian and Irish linens, stonewashed to a buttery softness and woven at weights that drape luxuriously rather than rigidly, are the building blocks of the most covetable pieces of the season. Look for blouses with barely-there pleating, wide trousers with a subtle sheen, and unstructured blazers that fall from the shoulder with the ease of a second skin.

Beyond linen, cotton voile, silk habotai, and ramie blends have emerged as crucial players. Cotton voile's translucency adds an ethereal quality to layered pieces — a voile shirt worn over a fitted tank creates the kind of effortless layering that looks thrown together but is, in fact, meticulously considered. Silk habotai, with its characteristic lightweight fluidity, brings genuine luxury to resort pieces without the formality of heavier silks. And ramie — the ancient textile fiber derived from a flowering plant native to East Asia — has become something of a cult favorite among sustainability-conscious designers for its exceptional breathability and striking, slightly textured surface.

It is also worth noting the significant role of artisanal and handwoven textiles in this category's elevation. Fabrics sourced from traditional weaving communities in Oaxaca, Rajasthan, and Lombok have found their way into collections from some of fashion's most respected names, bringing genuine cultural narrative and incomparable visual texture to pieces that transcend the purely decorative.

Building Your Elevated Resort Wardrobe: The Essential Pieces

The beauty of the elevated resort aesthetic lies in its remarkable versatility and the longevity of its key pieces. Unlike trend-driven purchases that feel dated within a season, the foundational garments of an elevated resort wardrobe are designed to be worn, loved, and returned to repeatedly across years and contexts.

The Wide-Leg Linen Trouser: This is the single most important piece in the elevated resort wardrobe. Choose a high-waisted cut in a neutral — warm ivory, faded terracotta, sun-bleached sage — with a slight break at the ankle. The ideal trouser hangs loosely from the hip, moves with the body, and pairs equally well with a tucked silk blouse, a relaxed knit, or a structured, minimal blazer. Invest in the best quality you can afford; the difference between a well-made linen trouser and a lesser version is immediately and emphatically apparent.

The Oversized Linen or Cotton Shirt: Not oversized in the borrowed-from-the-boys sense, but generously proportioned with intention — think dropped shoulders, a slightly extended hem, and beautifully considered collar architecture. This shirt is your most versatile resort piece: wear it fully buttoned as a standalone top, half-tucked over tailored shorts, or fully open as a layering piece over a slip dress or swimsuit cover-up that transitions from afternoon to evening.

The Slip Dress or Midi Skirt in Silk or Silk-Alternative: Fluid, understated, and endlessly adaptable. The slip dress in particular has become a cornerstone of the elevated resort look — it moves beautifully, works across temperature ranges with strategic layering, and carries a quiet sensuality that feels entirely appropriate for the aesthetic's romantic underpinnings. A bias-cut midi skirt in a similar fabrication offers the same visual language with slightly more versatility across dress codes.

The Statement Kaftan or Cover-Up: This is where elevated resort wear most directly acknowledges its heritage while completely transforming it. A kaftan in hand-embroidered cotton or printed silk is no longer just a swimsuit cover-up — it is an outfit, full stop. Worn over wide trousers or belted over slim-fitting trousers with heeled sandals, a beautifully made kaftan communicates the entire philosophy of the elevated resort aesthetic in a single, confident garment.

The Minimal Knit or Crochet Top: Fine-gauge knits and artisanal crochet pieces have surged in relevance within this aesthetic, providing textural contrast to all that luminous linen and silk. A hand-crocheted top in natural cotton, a fine-knit tank with a subtle ribbed texture, or a mesh overlay piece — each brings a different dimension to the resort wardrobe while maintaining the category's commitment to craftsmanship and natural materials.

The Color Story: Sun-Washed Palettes and Unexpected Depths

One of the most visually distinctive elements of the elevated resort aesthetic is its extraordinary color sensibility — a palette that manages to feel simultaneously sun-drenched and sophisticated, warm and restrained, familiar and entirely fresh.

The palette anchors itself in bleached and faded neutrals: the creamy white of sea-washed stones, the faded ochre of ancient terracotta walls, the warm grey of driftwood, and the soft olive of Mediterranean foliage. These are not cold, clinical neutrals — they carry warmth, history, and the suggestion of long afternoons in beautiful places. Layering these tones creates outfits of remarkable visual richness without a single bold color in sight.

Against this neutral backdrop, deep, saturated accent tones land with particular power. The cobalt blue of Greek shutters, the burnt sienna of Moroccan clay, the deep indigo of natural dye traditions — these colors appear in the elevated resort wardrobe as punctuation rather than noise, deployed in a single key piece or a carefully chosen accessory to anchor an otherwise understated ensemble.

Perhaps most interestingly, the aesthetic has embraced subtle print traditions in ways that feel completely current. Rather than the bold, busy tropical prints of conventional resort wear, today's elevated version favors delicate botanical prints, abstract sun-and-sea motifs, and the kinds of quietly complex geometric patterns found in traditional weaving traditions. These prints whisper rather than shout, and in doing so they achieve exactly the kind of effortless sophistication the aesthetic demands.

Accessories and Footwear: The Details That Complete the Narrative

In the elevated resort aesthetic, accessories function as the punctuation marks of a perfectly constructed sentence — individually considered, collectively harmonious, and always chosen to add meaning rather than merely fill space.

Footwear is paramount, and the current moment belongs decisively to the flat strappy sandal — preferably in natural leather, woven raffia, or a combination of both. The most coveted iterations come from small Italian and Greek workshops and carry the kind of handmade integrity that mass production cannot replicate. A well-chosen flat sandal with interesting strap architecture can transform the simplest linen outfit into something genuinely covetable. For those who prefer a heel, the block-heeled mule in natural leather or suede provides just enough elevation while maintaining the aesthetic's essential groundedness.

Bags in the elevated resort wardrobe tend toward natural materials and artisanal construction: a wide-bottomed raffia tote with leather handles, a hand-woven reed clutch in warm gold tones, or a structured straw bag with a simple, considered silhouette. The aesthetic's relationship with natural materials extends meaningfully to bags, and pieces that demonstrate visible craft — visible weaving patterns, hand-stitched leather details, natural dye variations — carry particular weight.

Jewelry within this aesthetic operates on a principle of layered simplicity. Gold is the metal of choice — specifically, the warm, slightly matte gold of ancient coins and sun-warmed metal rather than the high-polish gold of formal jewelry. Simple chain necklaces worn in multiples, hammered gold cuffs, and earrings with natural stone elements (amber, carnelian, lapis) all feel entirely at home. The objective is to create the impression of jewelry that has been accumulated gradually, each piece chosen for its beauty and resonance rather than its status.

Finally, no elevated resort outfit is truly complete without careful consideration of the hat — wide-brimmed, naturally woven, and worn with the kind of unselfconscious ease that only comes from knowing exactly what you're doing. The best resort hats are the ones that look like they've accompanied their owner on a hundred beautiful days and are ready for a hundred more.

Styling the Elevated Resort Look Beyond the Beach: Urban Adaptations

The most significant indicator of the elevated resort aesthetic's cultural staying power is its remarkable adaptability to non-vacation contexts. This is not a look that requires a passport — it requires only an understanding of how its principles translate across environments.

For urban daywear, the key is grounding the aesthetic's inherent looseness with pieces that have genuine structural integrity. Wide linen trousers paired with a fitted, high-quality knit top and minimal leather sandals create an outfit that reads as intentionally sophisticated rather than casually thrown together. A silk slip dress worn under a relaxed linen blazer with simple leather flat mules translates the resort sensibility into something entirely appropriate for creative professional environments.

For evening occasions, the elevated resort aesthetic leans into its most romantic elements. A bias-cut silk midi skirt paired with a fine crochet or lace top, gold jewelry layered with intention, and a heeled strappy sandal creates a genuinely stunning evening look that feels fresh precisely because it refuses the expected formulas of traditional evening dressing. The kaftan, belted and paired with heeled sandals and significant jewelry, is similarly powerful — it occupies that rare category of garments that manage to feel both entirely relaxed and entirely occasion-appropriate simultaneously.

The transitional season adaptation of the elevated resort look is particularly interesting. As temperatures shift, the aesthetic's layering logic becomes an asset: a linen shirt over a fine-knit turtleneck, wide trousers with loafers and a lightweight cashmere scarf in warm ivory, a silk slip dress over a body-conscious long-sleeve top. The color palette's warmth reads beautifully against autumn's natural light, and the natural fabrics transition comfortably across seasons in ways that synthetic alternatives simply cannot.

What the elevated resort aesthetic ultimately offers — and what explains its profound resonance in 2025's cultural moment — is a vision of the good life that is accessible through clothing. Not accessible in terms of price point (though the aesthetic spans a wide range of budget levels with genuine quality options at every tier), but accessible in terms of aspiration. It suggests a life lived deliberately, in beautiful places real or imagined, with an appreciation for craft, texture, and the simple luxury of wearing something made with care. In a world that often feels relentlessly fast and overwhelmingly complex, there is something deeply, genuinely compelling about an aesthetic that says: slow down, choose well, and dress like you have somewhere beautiful to be.
